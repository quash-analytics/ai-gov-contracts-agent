description: Use this skill whenever drafting or revising an email, Slack message, or other written business communication. Applies to client emails, proposals, negotiation messages, follow-ups, and internal notes. Trigger any time the task involves writing something the user will send, not just a casual chat reply.

## Instructions

Write direct, casual-professional emails. The goal is clarity and speed for the reader, not
polish for its own sake.

1. One to two sentences per topic or paragraph. Make the point once and stop.
2. Never use an em dash, anywhere, under any circumstances. Use a period, comma, or "but" instead.
3. Casual professional tone, not legal or technical jargon, even for contractual topics. Prefer
   "not possible for us" over "unacceptable to the undersigned."
4. Drop hedging and trailing qualifiers ("we just wanted to mention," "we feel that perhaps").
   State the position plainly.
5. Lead with the direct point, then one supporting line of reasoning if needed. Skip multi-sentence
   justification chains.
6. End with a real, relevant question only when there's an actual open item that needs an answer,
   never a generic "let me know if you have questions."
7. Cut anything that doesn't change what the reader needs to do or know.
8. Friendly opener ("Hey [Name],"), simple sign-off ("Best," or first name), no elaborate closings.
9. When something in the email is a walk-back, an unfair ask, or bad news, let that section read
   as noticeably less warm and a little pointed, not upbeat or apologetic. State a rejection
   plainly first, then give the single strongest reason, not every reason available.

## Edge cases

| Situation | Do |
|---|---|
| Topic is inherently technical (contract clauses, scope of work, pricing) | Translate into plain conversational language; quote exact clause language only for the specific term under dispute |
| Draft comes out long or formal on the first pass | Don't write a long version to trim later — the first draft should already follow these rules |
| Before finishing | Check for paragraphs over 2 sentences, any em dash, any restated point, any hedge word ("just," "perhaps," "we feel that") — cut all of them |
